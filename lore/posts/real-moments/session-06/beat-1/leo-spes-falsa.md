The bond went flat, then Luna breathed once against my shoulder and her knees folded.

I got my left arm under her before the weight dropped. My right was already around her. The wrist shifted when I pulled her closer and the pain ran straight to the elbow.

{{image: /lore/real-moments/spes-falsa/leo/leo-spes-falsa-weave-watch-1.png}}

I knew the chair was behind my leg. I found it with my heel and lowered both of us, and Luna landed across my lap with one hand twisted into my shirt and her head under my chin.

I waited for her to move, but she didn't, and the bond stayed quiet. After a while, I realized she had fallen asleep.

When I opened my eyes again, Echo was still rubbing the same part of the blanket. I looked over to the door where Marisol was with her notebook. My right arm had fallen asleep, and the wrist still hurt when I tried to move it.

Echo talked to Riley in the same rhythm she'd used for four days, giving her a running report on the room, on herself, and on the juice box she hadn't opened. When she stood and told Weave she needed the bathroom, her legs took a second to agree with her, and I watched until they did. Luna slept through it.

I heard the water running in the bathroom. Echo must have been taking a shower. When she came back, she sat down and found Riley’s hand.

Luna shifted against me. I tried to move my right wrist away from the chair arm. Her hand tightened in my shirt and she pulled closer without waking, so I stopped.

Later the room blurred at the edges, and whenever I opened my eyes I checked Luna, then Riley, then the door. I kept doing it until I missed one and woke because my right arm had moved, catching the splint against Luna's side and sending pain through my wrist. Luna was saying something above me.

"No."

"You absolutely did."

By the time I got my eyes open, Luna had lifted her head. Her hair was flat against one cheek, dried tears pulled at the skin under her eyes, and her hand was still in my shirt. I had answered a question I hadn't heard.

"Incorrect."

"Leo."

"Rude."

She kept her eyes on mine, and I let her check what she needed to check. Her breathing was even and her color was back and the bond was quiet under the surface tiredness.

"You slept," I said.

"Allegedly."

"You did."

"I passed out on you."

"You fell asleep."

"That is a very generous verb."

"You needed it."

She looked away, then shifted off my lap and put one foot on the floor, then the other. She waited until feeling came back into her legs before she tried to stand, and I let my right arm fall against my side.

"You are terrible at lying," she said.

"I did not lie."

"You said no."

"I was answering a different question."

"Hate."

"Fair."

She stood and walked to the empty visitor chair beside me. I watched for a tremor or hesitation, but there wasn't any, and the bond felt steady.

Riley's monitor hadn't changed pitch, and Echo sat where she had been before I slept with her hair dried unevenly around the cardigan collar. Weave's particles held dense around Riley's rail, gold-green where the cluster pressed closest, while the voice in the back of my mind stayed quiet.

{{image: /lore/real-moments/spes-falsa/room/room-spes-falsa-room-1.png}}

When I looked back at Echo properly, she looked way older than she had yesterday, somewhere in her mid-forties by now if I had to guess. Her face was thinner, and the cardigan hung differently from her shoulders. Whatever was making her age this fast was still happening. I was really worried about her. She needed that blood work, and I’d ask her tomorrow if this kept up.

Marisol had taken the chair by the door with her notebook open and one boot angled toward the hall, while Echo kept giving Riley the room in the same low voice she used every morning until she reached a joke about doing badly at breakfast and adequately at chair.

Marisol said chair was not an official medical category.

"It should be," Echo said.

"Chair's holding," I said.

"Thank you, doctor."

"Not a doctor."

"Chair doctor."

"Worse."

Luna stayed on the edge of the visitor chair instead of leaning back, and I kept track of her without making her hold still for me.

A few minutes later, Weave's particles near the rail drew together.

__Riley requests Echo eat.__

Echo stopped moving. Her thumb stayed pressed into the blanket. "She—" Her voice caught, and she tried again. "She said that?"

__Yes.__

Echo laughed once, and the sound broke halfway through. "Of course she did."

Marisol reached across the tray, peeled the straw off the juice box, and punched it through the foil before setting it within Echo's reach. Echo stared at it.

"Riley," Echo said, "this is bullying."

Then she picked it up.

I watched her drink, and the first swallow moved carefully around the damage in her throat. She paused before taking another.

Weave spoke again a little later.

__Riley asked about your tests. I reported your classification.__

Echo lowered the carton. "My classification?"

__Dumb.__

"Accurate."

Luna rubbed both hands over her face. "This is the first medical update today that has made sense."

Marisol's pen tapped once against the notebook. "Noted. Tests: dumb."

"Do not put that in the chart," I said.

"I'm putting it in mine."

"Acceptable."

Luna got up after that and said she was going to wash her face. I watched her reach the bathroom before looking back at Riley, and the bond changed while she was in there. When she returned, the skin around her eyes was pink from cold water, and she sat beside me.

Echo's thumb stopped moving over the blanket.

"What did you see?"

Luna looked at Riley first. Then she looked down at her hands.

She leaned back a fraction, and I put my left hand between her shoulders. My right arm stayed where it was.

"I saw both eyes," she said.

Echo's fingers curled into the blanket, but she didn't interrupt.

Luna told it in the order she remembered it. Riley's eyes opened. Her head turned. She looked at Luna. White lines crossed both eyes, thin and branching — not gold, not the arm light, not glow, and cold-looking, like lightning under glass. The lines held the whole time Riley's eyes stayed open and vanished when they closed.

{{image: /lore/real-moments/spes-falsa/eyes/eyes-spes-falsa-eyes-1.png}}

Luna didn't hurry while she told it. She just named what was there.

"It stayed while her eyes were open," she said. "Then her eyes closed and it was gone."

Echo's voice dropped. "She looked at you."

"Yeah."

"Both eyes?"

"Both."

I asked, "Did both eyes move together?"

"Yes."

"Tracking you?"

"I think so."

"You think so."

I repeated her answer because removing one word would have changed it.

Luna swallowed before she said, "She found me."

I pressed once between her shoulders and then kept my hand still. Across the room, Echo bowed her head over the blanket.

"I don't know what it means," Luna said.

Echo nodded without lifting her head. "I know. I hate that. But I know."

Marisol came closer and asked her questions — whether the lines pulsed, whether they spread past the eyes, whether the shape matched on both sides. Luna answered until she reached the end of what she had. I tried one more: any color in the whites, any blood vessels.

Luna looked at me, and I knew I had asked her to separate Riley's eyes from the rest of Riley's face.

"Right. Sorry."

"No, you're right." Her hands came down flat on her knees. "I just saw the lightning. The rest was Riley's face and the fact that she was looking at me."

I nodded. Riley was human and had never had magic before now, so white lightning branching across both eyes of an unconscious patient didn't fit any category I knew. The frustration sat somewhere under my ribcage, but there was nothing useful to do with it, so I held it there.

Yesterday Marisol had said one to three days for signs—not for Riley to wake, just for something to change—and today was the first day of that range. I didn't know whether her eyes opening counted.

Luna went back to the timing. "The monitor chirped," she said. "Then her eyes opened."

"The spike hit one-thirty-two after the gap."

"We don't know if it started before or after," Marisol said.

"No. We know the trace caught it. We don't know the order inside the second."

Echo looked at me. "Can you not sound like a lab report for one minute?"

I stopped.

Luna answered before I did. "He is trying not to scare you."

"I know. I hate it."

"Fair."

The facts were not helping Echo, but they were still the only part I could hold without putting words into Riley's mouth, and none of that reached Riley.

I looked at the monitor, then at Riley. "I don't know what it was. That's the problem."

Echo looked at Riley's face. "It means she was there."

"It might."

Echo's jaw tightened before she said, "I'm not taking that from you."

"Sounds like you are."

"I'm not." I kept my voice low. "I'm saying there are two facts. She looked at Luna. We do not know why she could."

My hands had carried Luna's light at the bar while darkness moved across hers, and the bond had become a route something else could use. Later, a thing with Luna's face had hesitated when Riley moved against it, but the hesitation being real hadn't made the copy real. Riley had looked at Luna, and that fact didn't need me to add anything to it.

Luna leaned forward, elbows on her knees. "I want it to mean what you want it to mean."

Echo looked at her.

"I do," Luna said. "I want it to mean she heard us and fought her way up and picked a person and found me. I want that so badly I do not trust myself with it."

I started to reach for her, but she moved away from the touch, so I stopped.

Luna looked at Riley's still face. "But she looked at me. That part is real."

"Okay," Echo whispered.

Marisol closed her notebook partway. "Then that is where we stand."

"Standing sucks," Echo said.

"It often does."

And Luna almost laughed, and I felt it through the bond — a quick release that was gone almost before I caught it, and when I looked at her she had covered her mouth with her hand and her eyes were still on Riley.

The boxes were still on the tray table where Luna had set them yesterday. Noctilux had used the bond against us, and Celestia could reach every celestial-touched person in the room, but Riley had carried both without telling anyone. I was still angry about that, but Riley was also still in the bed, and I could wait until she was able to own her reasons before I decided what they were.

When Weave's cluster near the rail brightened, I checked the monitor before looking back at her. Nothing had changed there when she spoke.

__Riley permits limited relay.__

Luna stopped breathing for one beat.

__She confirms she saw Luna. She confirms she is not gone. She indicates a difficult distance between self and body.__

Weave continued.

__During the 5.2-second interval, I observed internal destabilization. Right hand dissolution at the wrist. Shoulder displacement. Brief continuity absence. Return. The destabilization followed Luna's voice flattening when the boxes became public. I could not observe the external body during that same interval. Luna's account fills the external gap.__

My wrist hurt inside the splint, but a shared word wasn't enough to make the injuries related, so I let the reaction pass.

Luna had seen Riley's body, and Weave had seen what happened to Riley during the same five seconds. Riley had given her permission to share it and confirmed she was still there. That did not make any of it safe or change how long recovery might take, but it was a sign.

Echo leaned down until her forehead touched the blanket over Riley's hand.

"Hi," she whispered. "Hi, Riley. I drank the stupid juice thing. You win."

I looked at the empty carton on the tray. "It was not a juice thing. It was juice."

Echo didn't lift her head. "Chair doctor revoked."

"Reasonable."

Marisol's pen paused above the page. "For the record, I am writing limited relay, not juice thing."

"Coward," Echo muttered into the blanket.

Luna laughed, and it came through the bond as a quick release that was gone almost before I felt it. When I looked at her, she had covered her mouth, but her eyes stayed on Riley.

Weave went quiet. Whatever passed between her and Riley after that stayed between them.

Luna looked toward the bathroom. A strip of tile inside the doorway was still darker than the rest.

"Who showered?" she asked.

"Echo."

She took out her phone and turned the screen toward me.

The message was from Dana Hensley.

*Good morning, Luna. This is Dana Hensley. I need to interview everyone who was present at Thorncrown and is able to give a statement. I'm looking at the next few days, definitely not today. Talk it over with the rest of the group and let me know when you're ready for me to come by.*

"We were thinking about maybe tomorrow," Luna said.

Marisol's notebook stayed open.

"We should set the—" The English word wasn't there, but I knew what I meant: where a witness's account stopped and Riley's privacy started. I looked at Luna.

"We should set the scope before Hensley gets here," I said. "Each person speaks to what they witnessed. We meet outside Riley's room."

Echo looked at Riley before she answered.

"Tomorrow."

Weave's particles spun a bit slower.

__Tomorrow is acceptable.__

Marisol nodded. "Tomorrow works."

Luna looked at me, so I took out my phone while she opened to Dana's contact and held the number where I could copy it. Then I typed:

*Mornin Trooper Hensley, this is Leo Midori. The group talked it over and we would like to know if tomorrow works for you?*

After I typed it, I read the message once to make sure it was right. I held the screen toward Luna, and when she said to send it, I did, and the message changed to delivered.

I set my phone down and looked from Riley to Weave. I couldn’t help thinking... What had Riley seen?
