Luna is asleep on Leo.

She cried until she ran out of tears and then she kept crying without them for a while, and now she is asleep with her face pressed into his shirt and one hand caught in the fabric. Leo keeps both arms around her. The right one is not supposed to be doing that. The splint is a hard pale shape above her shoulder, and every time he moves that arm he gets too still afterward. I think it hurts him. Actually I know it hurts him, because I can see him do the thing where his face does not move but his halo shows.

The monitor over Riley's bed keeps its line. Same number. Same small beep. I have checked it so many times tonight that I can hear it before it happens. Or I think I can. Maybe I am just counting and the beep comes when I expect it, which is basically the same thing but also not. I do not know. Does not matter, probably.

My hand is on the blanket over Riley's wrist. Warm. Still warm. I rub the same place in the blanket until the fabric goes smooth there because I dont know what else to do. There is a worn spot now where I keep touching. I can feel it when I stop and start again. The fabric is soft there. Softer than the rest.

Marisol is by the door. Notebook closed on her knee. Boots flat. She has been at the door since we got here, and I have stopped knowing how many nights that has been. I think four. Maybe four. The days blur together in a way that means I should probably ask somebody to confirm that, but I keep not asking because the number does not change anything.

The boxes are back where they were put. Sealed. I do not look over there. I mean I do look. I looked when I sat down and I looked when I got up to go to the bathroom earlier and I looked just now while I was thinking about not looking. So I do look. I just wish I did not.

The juice box is still on the tray. The sandwich is somewhere. I stopped tracking it after the boxes came out.

Nothing in the room moves except the monitor and the vent. Well, and Weave. Weave is dense around Riley's bed rail, and some of her moves through the rest of the room in the slow circuit she has been doing all night. Monitor edge. Bed rail. Window. IV pole. Door. Riley. Then she starts over. I have been watching her do it long enough that I know the order, and I think maybe if the order changed I would know something was wrong. Or maybe I would not notice until it was already too late. I do not know which one is worse.

My skin starts bothering me. Dried tears pulling under my eyes. That tight scratchy feeling where the salt dried and the skin got stiff. Sweat where the cardigan sits against the back of my neck. Not a lot of sweat, just enough that the fabric feels wrong there, like it is stuck. The imaging-center smell in my hair, which is probably not a smell. Cold air and contrast dye and that machine banging. It is still in there somehow. I washed my hair and it is still in there. Okay.

I need to clean up. Actually I need to shower, I think. Wash my hair properly. Get the hospital off me. Or the imaging center off me, or both. Whatever it is that is still in my hair.

I dont want to take my hand off Riley.

Both thoughts keep happening, one and then the other, and I dont know how to do both. Take a shower and keep my hand on Riley's wrist. I cannot do both. I know I cannot do both. But I keep checking anyway like my brain is going to find a way to make two things that do not fit actually fit if I just keep going back and forth between them enough times. It does not work.

I look at Weave.

"I need to shower, Weave. May I?"

Her voice reaches me cleanly. Or clearly. It is clean and clear. Both.

__Would you like this one to watch the door for you?__

"Yes, please, thank you Weave."

I say it too quietly. Nobody else reacts, which is fine. I did not say it for them anyway. I said it for Weave. And maybe for Riley, even though she cannot hear me. Probably cannot. I do not actually know what she can and cannot hear, and I keep not asking Weave because I am afraid the answer is going to be nothing.

I trace the worn place once more. Just once. I find the soft spot in the blanket and I press it one more time. Then I lift my hand. The air against my palm is cold immediately. Cold and dry. Hospital air. I stand. Too fast. The room shifts half an inch and comes back. Leo looks at me, but Luna stays asleep against him and he does not move.

Riley is still breathing. The monitor is still the same.

I go. I hear Weave's particles shift around the room to the door.

The bathroom door sticks unless I pull it hard. It always sticks. Every time. I do not know why hospital bathroom doors stick but they do, and I have learned to pull from the bottom corner instead of the handle and that usually works better except tonight I dont want the noise, so I leave it open a crack instead. There is gold-green around the doorway after I step inside. Just enough that I can see Weave is there if I look down. She is right outside. That helps. I did not mean for her to stay that close but she did, and that means something. Probably something nice. I take it as something nice.

The bathroom is too bright. Every bathroom in this hospital is too bright. The light is the kind that makes everything look worse than it actually is, like the mirror is trying to pick a fight with whoever stands in front of it. I have been avoiding the mirror. Not on purpose, I mean I have been avoiding it on purpose but not in a planned way. I just do not look at it.

I fold the cardigan on the counter. One sleeve keeps falling off the side, so I fold it again and put the dress on top after I pull it off. The cardigan stays folded because the dress is heavy enough to hold it.

The room is cold without the clothes. My arms get goosebumps immediately. I was going to get straight in the shower. Except I am still standing here.

I dont look at the mirror.

The water takes forever to warm. I test it, turn it hotter, test it again, then it gets too hot because of course it does. I turn it back. The pipes knock in the wall and I freeze with my hand under the water until they stop. But I freeze anyway, and tonight is not different.

Then I get in.

The water hits the back of my head and runs down my spine. Cold first. Then warm. Then hot. I stand there longer than I mean to before I start washing, because the water on my back is the first thing tonight that has not hurt or itched or pulled or been wrong. It is just warm water. I stay under it until I feel like moving again.

My hair takes two rounds of shampoo. Or maybe one. I finish the first rinse and cant remember if I got the back properly, so I do it again. I mean I think I got it the first time. I probably did. But I do not remember, and doing it twice is fine.

The water hits the sore part of my throat. I turn away. My shoulder still hurts when I lift my arm high enough to rinse under it. Less than yesterday. Today it just feels like a bruise.

I wash the hospital smell off. Mostly. I think mostly. I will not know until I get out and my hair dries, so mostly has to be good enough.

The water is loud. I cant hear the monitor through it. I turn it down and then I can hear the water less and still cannot hear the monitor, which is worse. I rinse quickly and shut it off. The room goes quiet so fast my ears fill the space with ringing, that high noise that happens when something loud stops and your ears have to catch up. But the ringing means the water is off, and if the water is off I can hear the monitor.

I listen.

Beep.

Then another.

Okay. Okay, she is still there. The line is still the same. The line has been the same for days. I still check.

I dry my face and my hair. I press the towel around the ends because rubbing it will make it go everywhere, and once it is dry and frizzy I cannot fix it without a brush, and my brush is not in here. So pressing.

The mirror is covered in steam. It is all fogged up and I cannot see myself in it. I could get dressed right now without looking. I have been doing that. I have been not looking for days, since the road, since the residue, since Weave said approximately five years like it was a measurement and not my face.

But.

No. I should look. I have been not looking for days and it has not helped anything. It has just meant I do not know what I look like, and not knowing is worse, I think. Probably worse.

I wipe the mirror with my palm. I only clear the middle at first, because that is less scary than clearing the whole thing. One eye. Part of my cheek. Wet hair stuck to my skin. I wipe the rest.

Oh.

I look older.

Weave said approximately five years. She looked at me and her voice broke on it and the static around me was warm while she talked. She was trying to be gentle and it was not working. And I believed her because Weave does not say things she is not sure about.

I already knew. I just had not looked this long.

The face is mine. Same freckles. Same eyes. Same little mark near my chin that I have had since I was a kid. My bangs are split from the water and that shows too much forehead, and now I can see that my face has changed. It is more than the forehead.

I look a lot older.

Like... a lot.

The jaw is the thing I keep coming back to, because when you get older your jaw changes and I did not think about that before but now I cannot stop thinking about it. I turn my face and the skin near my eyes looks different. I did not get to see it happen. Five years with no pictures and no time where it moved slowly. One day I was me and the next day I was me plus five years. I do not know how it worked. Nobody knows what that clone was made of, or what the residue was exactly, or why it did this instead of something else. Maybe I should be glad it was only five years. It could have been worse.

I make the face I make when James says something that is technically correct and extremely annoying. It still looks like me doing it. Older me. I stop making the face.

The mirror light is awful. Fluorescent. I could blame some of it on that. I have not slept properly in days. I have not eaten. I cried until my eyes hurt. Any person would look different under those conditions. But I still look older.

I touch below my eye. The skin is thinner there. Or it feels thinner. Maybe it was always that thin and I am just noticing now because I am looking. I press two fingers along my jaw and turn the other direction. My knuckles look sharper. I think they do. The veins show more, but I was just in hot water, so that is probably just the hot water and not the five years. And I am probably dehydrated because the juice is still on the tray and I have not had any.

Did they take blood yesterday? They must have. There was an IV for days. They had blood, because hospitals take blood all the time and then they test it and nobody tells you what the tests said unless you ask.

Would regular blood work show whether I am still changing. Whether the five years is done. Whether there is more coming. Did anybody look for that. I do not know what you look for in blood when somebody has aged five years in one day from magical residue. Probably nothing. Probably the standard test is wait and see. The worst kind of test.

I look at the mirror again. It is the same face. Of course it is the same face. It has been five seconds. I checked anyway.

Maybe Weave knows. She said approximately five years, so she measured something. But she was barely holding together when she told me. I remember the warm static around me while she talked. Maybe she checked later. After things calmed down.

Leo might know if blood work would show anything. He would probably say he is not a doctor first and then he would still know which question to ask, because Leo knows which question to ask about everything. I should ask him. Not now. Luna is asleep on him and his wrist is broken and Riley—

I stop. The thought still feels rude in this room. Like worrying about my face when Riley is wherever she is.

I look at the mirror. Could it still be happening. Could the residue still be doing something to me right now and the next time I look I will be older again. I dont know. I really, really dont know. And I hate not knowing, because if I dont know then I keep checking.

I flex my fingers. They work. I turn my head. It works. My throat hurts. My shoulder hurts. Everything else is there and working and not changing as far as I can tell, but I could not tell the first time either. The first time I did not know it was happening until somebody told me.

I need to ask Weave or Leo about blood work, or about whether there is a way to check if it stopped. I repeat it in my head because I will lose it once I see Riley again. Ask about blood work. Ask if it stopped. Ask if the first hospital has a baseline. I am going to forget at least one of them.

I wipe another strip of steam from the mirror even though I can already see. There is nothing new to look at. I look anyway. I look older. A lot older. Okay. Not okay. Just... I have clothes to put on. That is the next thing.

I pull the dress over my head. It catches in my wet hair and I have to back out and try again because I got one arm through the neck hole. How did I even do that. The second try works. The cardigan feels heavy and familiar. I push both hands through the sleeves and pull it closed around me.

The mirror still looks weird with my normal clothes in it. My face is different but my dress is the same dress. The clothes did not change and I did.

I leave before I start checking again.

Weave is still near the threshold. More of her stays around Riley, exactly where I left her. The monitor is steady. Riley is steady. Leo still has Luna across his lap. Marisol has moved to her chair by the door, notebook closed. Nobody asks how long I took.

I sit and put my hand back on the blanket. My fingers find Riley's wrist immediately, right through the fabric. Still warm. Still the same. I rub the smooth place. The worn spot is still there. My thumb knows where it is without me having to look.

My hand looks older against the fabric now that I noticed it. I am going to keep noticing that. I leave it there anyway.

The night keeps going.

I watch the room fall asleep. One person at a time.

Luna's hand loosens in Leo's shirt and tightens again a few minutes later. She shifts her face against him. Her shoulder sits at an angle that looks uncomfortable, really uncomfortable, the kind of angle where you know somebody is going to wake up sore. But when Leo tries to make a little more space she pulls closer without waking up. He stops.

His wrist is tucked against her shoulder and the splint keeps catching the low room light, that pale medical plastic shining every time he shifts. Every time Luna moves in her sleep, Leo checks his wrist, then her, then Riley. He lasts longer than I expect. His eyes keep closing for one blink too many before he opens them again — looks at the monitor, looks at Luna, looks at the monitor again — and then his head rests against the back of the chair and he does not correct it. He is asleep. I count his breathing anyway. Ten breaths before I am sure.

I think about asking him now, about blood work, about whether it could still be happening. I think about it harder than is useful. He would wake up and answer. That is the problem with Leo. You ask him something at three in the morning while he is asleep on a hospital chair and he will wake up and give you a real answer, and then he will not be able to go back to sleep because his brain is already running. So I do not ask. Later.

Marisol is next. She looks at her phone with the brightness down until the light is so low that looking at it is basically the same as looking at nothing. She writes something in the notebook. Crosses it out. Writes it again. Then she puts the pen through the spiral and sets the notebook on her thigh. She checks the hall. Then Riley. Then the twins. Then me.

I look back until she stops looking at me. She does not, actually. She just closes her eyes while still facing the room. Her boots stay flat on the floor. One hand rests over the notebook. She looks like she could wake up already standing.

Marisol has not shifted, or at least I dont think she has. She is facing the door with her eyes closed now, but every few minutes they open. She checks the hall. Checks Riley. Checks all of us. Then closes them again. Is it rude to ask what species she is so I can learn more? Probably not, but maybe while she is trying to not-sleep is not the time. Maybe I did know and forgot.

A cart hits something in the hall. Marisol opens her eyes immediately. The sound is so small and she reacts like it was a door slamming. The sound moves away down the hall. She closes her eyes again. After that she sleeps, or whatever it is she is doing that is close enough to sleeping.

So everyone is asleep except me and Weave.

Riley is... Riley is wherever she is. I dont say asleep for her anymore. It feels wrong after Weave reached her.

I stay awake because I am already doing it. After a while, there is no decision in it. I just do not fall asleep. Monitor. Riley. Weave. Door. The twins. Back. Then I start over.

The juice box stays unopened. I pick it up once and the cardboard is cool and it is apple and I read the tiny nutrition panel because it is there — twenty-three grams of sugar, which seems like a lot, except I do not actually know how much sugar is supposed to be in juice. I put it down. My stomach feels empty in a way where I know I should eat but food sounds like too much texture and too much swallowing, and the swallowing part is the worst because my throat still hurts from crying. Later. When my throat does not hurt.

Weave is doing the circuit, the same one she has been doing all night — monitor edge, bed rail, window, IV pole, door, Riley, and then she starts over. She stops moving everywhere I can see her. She just stops. So I stop too and I wait and her particles start again after a few seconds and keep going around the room.

She used to be in three cars at once and she talked to us through all of them like it was easy, like being in three places at once was just a thing she did. But now part of her goes somewhere I cannot follow, and everything she leaves here gets quiet and thinner. The part that stays is still moving, still doing the circuit, still watching, but it is quieter than it used to be. I heard her voice break on *boxes*. She has not stuttered since, which should feel better, probably, except it does not. It feels the same as the stutter, and maybe worse, because at least the stutter meant she was reacting.

When I look at the twins, Luna makes a sound against Leo's shirt and tightens her hand in the fabric. His wrist is splinted and supposed to stay propped, which it is not — it is squished between her shoulder and the chair arm because he shifted it around her anyway. A thin gold ring blinks into place above his head and his closed eyes leak gold into the room, a soft glow spilling out from under his eyelids. He looks peaceful while it is happening, at least. The pain thing is helping him, I think, or whatever the ring is doing. I do not really understand the rings.

Luna moves her face against him, and I see her saying *hey... you* again. From before. From when Riley turned and the monitor jumped and everything went wrong. Except Luna is sleeping now. I am just seeing it again. I keep looking at Riley while I rub the blanket over her wrist and check the monitor again.

Weave goes still near Riley. Her particles pull tight around the rail, all of them drawing in close like something is happening on the other side.

I almost ask her about my face. The words are right there. But her particles tighten near Riley's head before I get the words out, and whatever she is doing with Riley right now is more important.

The room gets quieter and then louder in a different way — fewer visitors at this hour and more staff, rubber wheels rolling down the hall, low voices outside, doors clicking carefully so they do not slam. A nurse comes in and she tells Riley before every touch. She says blood pressure and she takes it. She says arm and she checks it. Wrist. IV — she checks the site and the line and the bag. I move my hand when she needs the space and I put it back when she finishes. She writes down the numbers on a clipboard.

I almost ask her about the blood work. The question gets as far as my throat and then it stays there. I dont know her. I would have to explain the road and the residue and five years, and I dont even know what lab I am asking for. Weave first. Or Leo. Somebody who already knows what happened.

The nurse leaves. I keep watching.

I close my eyes once. Just once. Riley turns. The monitor hits loud. Luna cries. The white lines web across both eyes. The mirror is there. My face in it. Riley's eyes were yesterday and the mirror was tonight. My brain put them next to each other anyway. I open my eyes. No sleep then. Fine.

I lean near the rail until my hair dries flat against one side of my head. It mats against the rail and I know it is going to look weird later but I do not move. The cardigan slips off one shoulder. I pull it up. It slips again later and I leave it. The coffee smell in the room gets old. The light outside the blinds goes from black to almost gray.

It is still Saturday. It will not be for much longer. I keep track of the day because if I lose the day, I lose the count too, and the count is the only thing keeping the days separate from each other. Sunday is coming. I do not want Sunday.

Luna changes before she wakes.

She catches a breath and holds it. Her fingers tighten in Leo's shirt. She frowns against his chest, just a little, the kind of frown that means something is happening inside her head that she does not like. Then she turns her face like she is trying to get away from something without waking up enough to know where she is going.

Leo stays asleep. Marisol stays asleep.

I stop rubbing the blanket.

A thin gold halo flashes above Luna's head. It is there all at once, appearing like it was always there and I just could not see it until now. Tilted with her head where she is lying against Leo. The ring throws a soft gold line across his face and catches on the edge of his splint. The light is warm. Not harsh. Warm like a lamp.

Then stars show up in the room.

Small points of light hanging in the air. Gold and pale. A few near the window and one over the end of Riley's bed. They drift, barely moving, just enough that I know they are not stuck to anything. One reflects in the monitor screen, a tiny gold dot against the green line.

I look at Weave. Her particles have stopped everywhere I can see them. So she sees it too.

Her halo brightens once. Just once. A pulse. Luna's face pulls tighter. Nightmare, maybe. Or a dream. I cannot tell from her face whether the thing inside her head is good or bad. But asleep faces do weird things, so maybe she is not upset. I am not waking her over one frown and some stars unless—

The stars disappear all at once. Pop. Gone. And her halo goes with them.

Luna breathes out and settles harder against Leo. Whatever it was, it is over.

I wait. Weave starts moving again. She gathers near the rail, a little tighter than before, then continues the circuit like nothing happened. But she saw it. I know she saw it because she stopped.

That was not regular stars. They were in the room, and Weave saw them too.

Then I remember something Leo told me a while ago. Luna fell asleep at a college desk once and soft-gold stars filled the classroom, but they disappeared before she woke up. I do not remember when he told me, only the stars.

I think these were the same ones because they disappeared before Luna woke up too. What was she dreaming about? Maybe the stars came from the dream, or maybe they were doing something around her while she dreamed. I dont know, so I rub the same place on Riley's blanket and try to keep the order straight in case I have to explain it later.

The gray outside the blinds keeps coming. Sunday starts without asking permission, the way it always does. The hall gets louder. That morning shift change noise. The coffee smell gets replaced by fresh coffee and then goes stale again almost immediately.

Luna wakes first. She surfaces slow, in pieces. Her hand tightens and then loosens and then tightens again. Her breathing changes. She looks like she is coming back from somewhere far away. Leo wakes when she moves. His eyes open before he is fully awake, which means his body knew she was moving before his brain caught up.

They do the wrist thing again. The one where he swears he did not move it and she counts the ways he is lying. He says no. She says you absolutely did. He says incorrect. She says Leo. He says rude. It is the same argument every time.

Marisol is awake by then. Notebook in her lap. Still facing the room. She does not look like she slept. Not really. She looks like she rested her body in a chair for a few hours while her brain kept working.

I look at Riley's face.

"Morning," I say. "Still here."

My voice is rough. It is better than it was on the road, back when it was basically gone. But worse than normal. Still scratchy. Still sounds like I spent yesterday crying.

I give her the small report. Luna slept. Leo stayed. Marisol is still doing the door thing. Weave is doing Weave things. The room did not fall apart overnight. I leave out the stars on purpose.

Then, because I am already talking and it feels weird to just stop, I keep going. "I am doing badly at breakfast but pretty okay at chair."

"That is not an official medical category," Marisol says. She does not look up from her notebook but I can hear the almost-smile in her voice.

"It should be."

Leo looks at the monitor. "Chair's holding."

"Thank you, doctor."

"Not a doctor."

"Chair doctor."

"Worse."

I almost laugh. I do not, but I almost do, and that is closer than I have been in days.

Weave goes still near Riley. That specific kind of still where I know something is about to happen.

Then her voice reaches the whole room.

__Riley requests Echo eat.__

I freeze.

The mirror is there. My face in it. Older. Blood work and baselines and whether it stopped. I push it down because I am not going to make this about my face when Riley just spoke.

My hand stops on the blanket. For a second everything stops.

Riley just asked me to eat.

The juice box. Of course. Of course Riley gets one clear route into the room and she uses it to find the exact thing I have been avoiding since last night. Whether I ate.

"She—" My throat closes. I swallow. It hurts. "She said that?"

__Yes.__

I laugh once. It comes out weird. Wet and kind of offended and also kind of grateful.

"Of course she did."

Marisol steps to the tray. She peels the straw off the side of the box, pushes it through the foil, and sets it close enough that I do not have to reach far. She does not say anything. She just does it.

I stare at the box.

"Riley," I say, low, "this is bullying."

The monitor does nothing. Riley does not respond either, and that feels like an answer. Like she is letting the accusation stand.

I pick up the box. The straw touches the sore place at the back of my mouth wrong. I adjust it. Drink twice. The first swallow hurts going down. The second is easier. The juice is cold and exactly apple. And it helps. I do not want it to help. I want to be mad that Riley made me eat when she is the one in the bed. But the juice helps and I keep drinking it anyway. I keep my other hand on Riley's wrist while I drink.

After a while Weave's voice reaches me again.

__Riley asked about your tests. I reported your classification.__

I lower the box.

"My classification?"

__Dumb.__

I look at Riley's covered hand. At the blanket over her knuckles. At the place where my thumb has worn the fabric smooth.

"Accurate."

It is funny for half a second. Then it catches on the mirror again. Tests. The blood work. Did they already do the thing I need. Do the tests include whatever I am trying to ask about.

Luna rubs both hands over her face. "This is the first medical update today that has made sense."

Marisol taps her pen once against the notebook. "Noted. Tests: dumb."

"Do not put that in the chart," Leo says.

"I'm putting it in mine."

"Acceptable."

I look at Leo. I should ask him now. He is awake. I could ask him about blood work and whether it stopped and whether there is a baseline from the first hospital. But Riley is talking through Weave and I am not going to change the subject to my blood because Riley asked about my tests. I take another drink of the juice instead. I know I am avoiding the question. I know. Doing it anyway.

Weave's attention keeps moving somewhere I cannot follow. The particles I can see hold closer to Riley for long stretches, denser around the rail. Then they loosen and spread. Nobody interrupts her. We all just wait.

I talk to Riley until I run out of safe things. James came to visit. The family came. Mom and Dad were here. The gun is handled. Nobody is going to touch the gun. The room did not fall apart while I was gone. The boxes are sealed. Nobody opened them. I stop there because the boxes are not safe.

Luna looks more rested around the edges and worse everywhere else. Her eyes are still pink. Her hands are doing that thing where they do not know where to rest. She keeps looking at Riley and then away and then back.

I want to ask Luna what she was dreaming about. The stars keep showing up in my head even though they are gone. Except then I would have to tell her I saw them. I would have to say I was watching her while she slept, and that sounds creepy even though I was watching everybody while they slept. The words come out before I decide which question I am asking.

I stop rubbing the blanket.

"What did you see?"

Luna looks at Riley first. Then she looks down at her hands.

Leo's left hand goes between her shoulders. His right wrist shifts against his side. His halo flickers once and is gone. The gold leaks from under his eyelids for half a second and then stops. He gets too still afterward, the way he does when he is hurting and does not want anyone to notice. I notice anyway.

"I saw both eyes," Luna says.

Both eyes.

Oh.

"Her eyes opened. Fully. Her head turned. She looked at me."

I breathe in too fast. Both eyes. Both eyes opened. Her head turned. She looked. Riley was there, behind her eyes, and Luna was the only one on that side of the bed.

"It wasn't just one eye," Luna says. "I saw both. White branching lines across both of them. Hair-thin. Like lightning under glass. Not gold. Not the arm light. Not glow. Cold-looking."

The stars last night were gold. Luna's halo, gold. Leo's eyes leaking gold out from under his eyelids. The lightning in Riley's eyes was not gold at all — it was white and cold and nothing like the gold anywhere else in this room, which I should probably stop trying to make things match.

"It stayed while her eyes were open," Luna says. "Then her eyes closed and it was gone."

"She looked at you."

"Yeah."

"Both eyes?"

"Both."

I press my lips together. Both eyes. Riley opened both eyes and found Luna. Or her head turned and her eyes moved and Luna happened to be in the direction her head turned. Leo is going to say the second one. I know he is. Somebody has to hold the line between what we want and what we know.

Leo's voice is careful when he asks it. "Did both eyes move together?"

"Yes."

"Tracking you?"

"I think so."

"You think so."

Luna's throat tightens. "She found me."

I lower my head.

She found me.

She looked at Luna. That is true. I cannot get any further with it.

Blood vessels. Blood work. The question comes back, the one about whether I am still changing. Not now. Not while Luna is still holding the lightning behind her eyes.

Leo says something about the trace catching the spike but not the order inside the second. I do not fully follow it.

Leo blinks. I caught him off guard. Good.

"I don't know what it was. That's the problem."

The answer hits wrong. I know Leo is being careful because being careful is what he does when the facts do not add up.

"I'm not taking that from you." My voice comes out sharper than I meant it to.

"Sounds like you are."

Two facts. She looked at Luna. We do not know why she could. I want her to have meant it. I want the look to mean she was reaching for us. I do not know if that is what happened.

Luna leans forward. Elbows on her knees.

"I want it to mean what you want it to mean."

"I do. I want it to mean she heard us and fought her way up and picked a person and found me. I want that so badly I do not trust myself with it."

Oh.

Luna wants it too.

Leo starts to move his hand, then stops and waits. He does not touch her. He just puts his hand where she can reach it if she wants to.

"Okay."

Weave brightens at the rail again. Brighter this time.

__Riley permits limited relay. She confirms she saw Luna. She confirms she is not gone. She indicates a difficult distance between self and body.__

She saw Luna.

Luna did not imagine it. Riley confirmed it. She is still in there, somewhere, making choices about what reaches us.

__During the 5.2-second interval, I observed internal destabilization. Right hand dissolution at the wrist. Shoulder displacement. Brief continuity absence. Return. The words land out of order. I catch *dissolution* and then *return* and I grab *return* with both hands, because return means she came back. But *absence* is before that. And *dissolution* is before that. Her hand came apart at the wrist. Her shoulder moved wrong. She was absent for five point two seconds. Not gone. Absent. Weave used the word she meant.

I look at Luna. She has closed her eyes. Leo is completely still. Marisol's pen is touching the page without moving.

Riley saw Luna. Riley is not gone. There is a distance. Those are the three things. She could have told us anything.

I stand up. My legs are stiff. I do not care.

I lean over the rail. I tuck her hair behind her ear. It falls the same way it always has. I cup her cheek. Warm. Still warm.

She is right here. Her body is right here and Weave says there is a distance and I do not know what to do with that. I do not know how to close a distance I cannot see or measure. But she is right here and my hand is on her cheek and she is warm.

I keep my hand there... Hoping soon...
