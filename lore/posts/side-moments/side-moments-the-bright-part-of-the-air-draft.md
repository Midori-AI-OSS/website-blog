# The Bright Part of the Air (Uncleaned Draft)

Riley had been inside the trade hall for forty-three minutes when she stopped pretending the crowd was manageable.

The ceiling was too high for the noise to settle anywhere. Every demonstration bell, sales pitch, burst of applause, and badly tuned focus test climbed into the steel rafters and came back flatter and louder. The aisles had looked generous on the event map. In person, people filled them shoulder to shoulder, carrying staff cases, shopping bags, folded cloaks, sample boxes, and drinks with decorative crystal in the cups.

Riley kept one hand hooked under the strap of her crossbody bag whenever someone brushed close enough to shift it. Her sweater was already warmer than the building deserved. The black roots of her hair stuck lightly to her temples before the color fell loose over her shoulders, blue deepening through the length and fading to cyan at the ends. Every few minutes she pushed the same section away from the hinge of her rectangular glasses. It came back.

Echo walked beside her with her brass staff planted lightly ahead of each step. The staff looked like something pulled from a careful workshop wall: worn gold metal, plain seams, a small crystal at the top shifting between the colors set into the belt at her waist. Her black dress brushed mid-calf. A sage-green cardigan hung open over it. Warm copper-gold hair framed her face in a blunt bob, and she kept turning her head away from the nearest speaker stack before her body caught up and followed.

“You picked the center aisle,” Echo said.

“I picked the aisle marked accessible and wide.”

“It lied.”

“Yea.” Riley shifted sideways around a man carrying a six-foot box labeled ETHICALLY SOURCED WYRM GLASS. “I feel personally betrayed.”

Echo’s mouth moved at one corner. Her shoulders stayed high.

The twins were twenty feet ahead, easy to lose and then easy to find again once Riley caught the deep green of their hair. Luna had chosen a cream shirt under a long charcoal cardigan, tan pants, and black tennis shoes. Her deep-blue spellbook rode against her side, clipped like a planner she expected to need every six minutes. Leo wore a dark shirt under a loose midnight jacket, dark chinos, and sturdy shoes. His brown leather book looked used enough to have survived years of students, weather, and being set down beside coffee.

They were the same height, both compact and slight, their long hair falling in loose waves with mossy color at the tips. At a distance Riley still read Luna first by movement. Luna leaned closer when she listened. Leo turned his whole body toward whatever had become his responsibility.

At that moment his responsibility was a vendor explaining a set of engraved containment tiles.

Luna looked over the vendor’s shoulder toward Riley and lifted two fingers.

Riley lifted hers back.

The vendor struck one tile with a tuning fork.

A clear note rang through the aisle. Gold lines traveled outward across the tile in a perfect square. Three people applauded. Somebody behind Riley dropped a metal cup. A child laughed near the next booth. A demonstration phoenix released a puff of warm orange sparks under a netted canopy.

The whole hall was doing too many things at once.

Riley stepped toward the twins.

Across the aisle, a pale-gold display began to hum.

The booth had been busy since they came in. Riley remembered that because the display case sat too close to the aisle and forced everyone into a narrowing between two tables. The centerpiece was a clear column filled with fine golden grit, lit from beneath. A ring of smaller crystals floated around it in a slow orbit while a presenter in a silver vest spoke into a headset.

His words vanished under a sharp crack from somewhere inside the pedestal.

The floating crystals stopped.

Every one of them turned point-first toward the center column.

The column flashed white.

The clear casing came apart with a dry sound, more like a bag bursting than glass breaking. Pale-gold powder lifted in a tight plume, hung for one impossible second, and spread sideways through the booth.

The crowd made a single backward movement.

Someone screamed.

The powder reached the aisle and kept going.

It moved too evenly. Each grain caught the overhead light, then caught it again, brightening as it passed through the first row of people. The air above the booth rippled in concentric circles. Another pulse traveled from the cracked pedestal. The dust rose higher.

Riley smelled hot stone and something clean enough to hurt.

Luna’s head snapped toward the booth.

Leo was already looking at her.

Thin gold rings appeared above both their heads at the same instant.

They came in solid and bright. Luna’s warm gold eyes lit until the color flattened into light. Leo’s did the same. The rings threw clean gold across their still-loose hair and pale, freckled faces.

“Celestia,” Luna said.

“Powder,” Leo answered.

The feathers flickered in and out, each return brighter, and the people behind her began shouting because she had blocked the nearest exit.

A man in a dark suit turned toward the shouting. Pale fire gathered around his hands. He stepped between the winged woman and a group of children, facing the woman as if she were already attacking them.
