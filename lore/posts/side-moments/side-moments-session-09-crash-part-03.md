Readers who find emergency medical care or enforced separation distressing may prefer to skip this installment.

The firefighter followed my hand to the board.

“Okay,” he said. “I’ll bring it to you.”

He moved it the last few inches instead of asking me to move the last few inches.

Bless him.

I kept my palm flat on the orange plastic while he crouched beside it. My fingers were shaking. I watched them for a second because they were easier than looking at everything else.

“Luna, I’m going to get this under you,” he said. “We’ll keep your right leg where it is.”

“Tell me before hands.”

“I will.”

Leo sat against my left shoulder. He looked worse now that I could hold his face in my head for more than half a second at a time.

Dried blood at his hairline. Dirt along one cheek. That awful careful breathing I knew from years of watching him hurt.

He caught me staring.

“I’m here,” he said.

“I can see you.”

“Then keep looking.”

Fine.

I looked.

The bond between us was rough and tired. Under that, Leo was scared.

Deep scared.

It sat low in my chest where his fear always landed, close enough to mine that I had to stop for a second and figure out which breath belonged to me.

A responder crossed between us and the ambulance.

When he moved, Echo came back into view.

I let go of the board.

Blood had soaked the green knit at her side until it looked almost brown under the lights. The black underneath shone wet where the fabric had shifted. Her face had gone gray around the mouth. One hand still rested over the medic’s at her side, but she barely seemed to have hold of him anymore.

And there was color around her.

A few tiny prismatic points drifted close to her shoulder and hair. Violet. Green. Gold. One little red flash that vanished when she moved.

My stomach dropped.

“Echo.”

She looked past me first.

Then found me.

“Luna?”

So quiet.

Fuck.

“Get her farther away.”

The firefighter beside me looked over.

“They’re taking her to the unit.”

“Faster.” My voice caught. “Please.”

One of the medics carrying Echo heard me and looked toward Marisol.

Marisol stood down by the van, several yards beyond us. She raised one hand.

“Keep moving,” she called. “Straight to the unit.”

The medic nodded. He said something to his partner and they picked up their pace without jarring the board.

Echo disappeared behind the ambulance.

I shoved my left palm into the road.

Leo caught my sleeve.

“Luna.”

“I just need to see her.”

I pushed.

I came up maybe an inch.

My right leg shifted against the pavement.

Everything went white.

I heard myself make a sound. Somebody caught me under the left arm. Leo kept hold of my sleeve. The firefighter steadied my shoulder until I stopped trying to move because I had forgotten how.

Road.

Orange board.

Leo.

Breathe.

“Luna.”

“I’m here.”

My voice sounded far away.

Leo loosened his grip but kept his hand on my sleeve.

“They have her,” he said. “Let them get her out of here.”

I stared toward the ambulance.

“I left her there.”

Leo opened his mouth. Closed it again.

I looked back toward the wreck.

Echo had been in that car while I fought people trying to help us. While I watched Leo. While every step away from the car had felt wrong enough to make me want to crawl back on my hands.

She had been bleeding the whole time.

I had seen it.

I had still almost missed those little colors around her.

“Luna,” Leo said again.

I swallowed.

“Yeah.”

Weave pressed along the outside of my left knee.

__here__

I put my hand into the edge of her cloud.

“Yeah, sweetheart.”

The firefighter waited until I looked at him again.

“Can I move you onto the board now?”

I looked at Leo.

He gave me a tired little nod.

“Do it.”

They told me where every hand was going.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Under my thigh.

Someone took my lower leg and the world disappeared again.

When it came back, I was on the board with a strap across my hips and Leo saying my name from somewhere close.

I turned toward him.

“There.”

“There what?”

“You came back.”

“Oh.”

That scared me more than I wanted it to.

I looked away from his face before he could see how much.

At the first ambulance, Riley was already inside. I could see part of her through the open rear doors: her glasses still on, two medics working around her.

Echo’s board came up alongside it.

Echo turned her head toward Riley as they brought her past.

“Riley?”

A medic leaned close. “She’s right here.”

Echo tried to lift herself enough to see.

She got halfway and sank back.

“Here,” she whispered.

“We’ve got you.”

Echo kept looking toward Riley.

She didn’t look at me again.

Fine.

Look at Riley. Keep looking at Riley.

The little prismatic pieces around her had thinned. I caught one violet point near her hair and then lost it behind the medic’s shoulder.

A medic reached for the rear door.

“Wait.”

Too many people looked at me.

I hated that immediately.

“Tell them where we’re going.”

“Same hospital,” the medic by the door said. “All of you.”

Echo moved her head a little.

“Same?”

“Same hospital.”

“Okay.”

Her eyes closed.

The doors shut on Riley.

Her ambulance pulled away.

Echo’s board kept moving toward the next unit.

I watched until another vehicle blocked Riley’s ambulance.

My chest hurt in a place none of the medics had touched yet.

Leo breathed beside me.

I listened to him instead.

Anita came around the van carrying two spellbooks.

Mine first.

Leo’s tucked underneath it.

She got close enough to hand them off, looked above my face, and stopped for one beat.

Then Anita gave my spellbook to the firefighter beside me.

“This stays with her.”

“Yes, ma’am.”

She passed Leo’s over next.

“And this one with him.”

“Got it.”

Anita stepped back as soon as his hands were empty.

She went to the van and opened the rear door. Moved something inside. Closed it. Opened it again a few seconds later and kept sorting through whatever was back there.

I looked away.

The paramedic beside me told me he was checking my ribs.

I said okay.

He warned me before touching me.

I still jerked when he pressed the wrong place.

“Sorry.”

“Warn me again.”

“I will.”

Across from us another paramedic worked with Leo.

I caught pieces while mine kept asking me to breathe deeper than I wanted to.

“Lost consciousness?”

“I don’t know.”

“Neck pain?”

“Yes.”

Leo sounded tired instead of chopped into little pieces now.

That helped.

The paramedic beside me pressed my ribs again.

Pain stole whatever happened after that.

I came back on a cot.

Metal under my shoulders.

Straps at my hips and leg.

Weave packed along my left side.

Leo was on another cot a few feet away.

I had missed them moving him.

“Leo.”

He turned toward me.

“I’m here.”

I pulled one breath all the way in.

“Okay.”

A responder adjusted the splint against his right arm and secured it closer to his chest. Leo watched the whole thing.

Marisol came up the shoulder toward us.

She stopped several feet from the foot of my cot and looked past us toward the transport units.

Then toward the wreck.

Then back to the paramedics.

“Those are their units?” she asked.

“Yes.”

Marisol nodded.

“Before you move either of them, secure Luna’s arms and Leo’s left arm, please.”

I stared at her.

“Excuse me?”

Marisol looked at me.

Her face softened for half a second.

“I know.”

“That is a terrible sentence.”

“I’m sorry.”

The paramedic beside my cot glanced toward the ambulance, then back at Marisol.

“You think they’ll fight the move?”

“Yes.” Marisol rubbed two fingers against her thumb, then stopped doing it. “Keep talking to them if they do.”

Nobody argued with her.

That bothered me.

I turned toward Leo.

He was already looking at me.

The fear in the bond had gotten heavier while Marisol talked.

I reached across the gap.

Leo reached with his left hand and caught mine.

Cold fingers.

Familiar fingers.

There.

I squeezed him hard enough that he squeezed back.

“You okay?” I asked.

“No.”

“Same.”

His mouth twitched and gave up.

Marisol waited until we both looked at her.

“They need to secure you now.”

I hated it.

I hated the cots.

I hated the ambulances.

I hated the empty road where Riley and Echo had disappeared.

Mostly I hated letting go of Leo.

He squeezed my hand once more.

“I’m staying right here,” he said.

Something in my chest pulled tight.

“You better.”

“I will.”

We let go.

The paramedic folded my left arm down beside me and secured it. Then my right. Enough room to move my hands. No room to sit up and use them for anything useful.

Across the gap, another responder secured Leo’s left arm against the cot.

Leo watched him do it.

He stayed still.

Weave stretched between us, gold-green particles thinning across the gap.

Then she separated.

A smaller cloud gathered beside Leo’s shoulder. The rest settled near my hip.

__present__

Leo looked at his half.

“Yeah.”

I looked at mine.

“Bossy.”

The paramedic at my feet checked the lock on my cot.

“Who goes first?” someone asked.

“Luna,” Marisol said.

My whole body tightened.

“No.”

Leo turned toward me.

“Luna.”

“You go first.”

“I’m staying here until you’re in.”

“That’s worse.”

“I know.”

The fear under his voice hit the bond hard enough to make my stomach turn.

He was scared.

Really scared.

And they were about to roll me away from him.

I pulled against the straps before anybody touched the cot.

The paramedic beside me put his hand on my shoulder without pressing down.

“Luna. We’re moving you first. Leo stays right here until you’re loaded.”

I looked at Leo.

He nodded.

“I’m staying put.”

I believed him.

That should have helped more than it did.

The cot started moving.

At first it was only wheels over rough pavement.

Leo stayed where he was.

I kept looking at him.

One yard.

Then another.

The wreck came back around the side of a fire truck.

Broken metal under hard white light.

Glass across the road.

My skin prickled.

I lost track of what the paramedic was saying.

Leo got smaller behind me.

No.

“Stop.”

The cot kept moving for another half second, then stopped.

“What do you need?”

“Leo.”

“He’s still there.”

I could see that.

That wasn’t enough.

The fear in him pressed hard through my chest.

I had to get back to him.

Simple.

Get to Leo.

“Turn me around.”

“Luna—”

“Turn me around.”

Leo raised his voice from down the shoulder.

“I’m here.”

I pulled against the straps.

The one across my left arm caught first.

I hauled against the straps and tried to sit all the way up.

Left foot to the road. Stand. Get back to Leo.

The paramedic caught my shoulder.

“Luna, stay down.”

“I need him.”

“He’s right there.”

Leo’s fear hit again.

Deep. Sharp. Mine answered it before I had a thought ready.

I shoved against the straps and dragged my left foot toward the road.

My right leg moved with me.

Pain tore straight through everything.

My body dropped back onto the cot.

I screamed.

The paramedic braced me through the shoulder while another grabbed the rail and kept the cot still.

Somebody said my name.

I couldn’t find whose voice it was.

Leo.

I needed Leo.

“Leo!”

“I’m here!”

His voice came back immediately.

I turned toward it.

He was still on his cot.

Still where he said he would be.

His left arm was secured. He had gone pale enough that I could see it even under the lights.

The fear in the bond was huge now.

I hated him being scared.

I hated being this far away.

I hated every person between us even though some part of me still knew they were helping.

“Bring him.”

“We will,” the paramedic said.

“Now.”

“Luna.”

“Now.”

Marisol’s voice came from behind the cot.

“Leo is staying where he is until you’re loaded.”

I looked for her and found her several feet away.

She kept her hands down at her sides.

“Then stop taking me away from him.”

“I’m sorry.”

Her voice stayed quiet.

That almost made me angrier.

Leo called my name again.

I looked back at him.

“I’m staying put,” he said. “Let them load you.”

“Leo—”

“I’m here.”

His voice shook on the last word.

Fuck.

I could feel how scared he was.

He was scared. I had to get back to him.

I pulled against the straps again.

The paramedic kept one hand at my shoulder and waited until I stopped wasting strength against something I wasn’t getting out of.

“Can we move?” he asked.

I stared at Leo.

Leo nodded once.

I wanted to say no.

I heard myself say, “Slow.”

“Slow,” the paramedic agreed.

They moved me again.

The closer we got to the ambulance, the harder it was to keep anything except Leo in my head.

A medic opened the rear doors.

Leo.

The cot turned.

Leo.

The medic checked the frame.

Leo.

They lifted the front wheels.

“Leo.”

“I’m here.”

My cot rolled head-first into the ambulance.

The ceiling swallowed the sky.

The rear opening dropped farther toward my feet as they pushed me in. I twisted as far as the straps let me.

White wall.

The medic.

A sliver of road.

No Leo.

I hauled against both arm restraints. The cot shook under me. The medic climbing in beside me caught my shoulder before I could wrench myself sideways.

“Leo!”

“He’s still outside. They’re moving him next.”

“I can’t see him.”

“I know.”

“Get me out.”

“Luna, stay with me.”

“Get me out!”

I tried to sit again.

Pain from my leg folded me back before the straps had to.

I couldn’t get enough air.

The medic stayed where I could see his face.

“Leo is being moved now.”

I listened.

Engines.

Radios.

Metal.

Somebody outside calling a count.

Then Leo’s voice, farther away than I wanted it.

“Luna.”

I went still long enough to hear him.

“I’m here!”

“I know.”

He sounded scared as hell.

He also sounded like Leo.

“Stay there,” I shouted.

“I am.”

“Don’t get up.”

“I’m not.”

The medic beside me looked toward the open doors.

I didn’t care what he thought about any of this.

Outside, wheels started over the pavement.

The fear in the bond climbed with every scrape and bump I could hear.

My hands curled around nothing because the restraints kept my arms down.

I had to get to him.

I had to get to him.

“Where is he?”

“They’re loading him now.”

“Tell me when he’s in.”

“I will.”

Weave crowded close against my hip.

__present__

“I know.”

My voice cracked.

Outside, somebody said, “Easy, Leo.”

Then Leo said, very clearly, “I’m staying still.”

Of course he was.

He had told me he would stay put. He was doing it.

My eyes burned.

“Come on,” I whispered.

The medic heard me.

“He’s almost in.”

I pulled against the restraints again when the bond spiked.

Then something changed.

The fear in Leo dropped so fast I sucked in a breath.

My own hands stopped fighting the straps.

The need to get off the cot was still there for a second, huge and stupid and right in front of me.

Then it loosened.

I could feel the vinyl under my palms.

The strap across my forearm.

The medic’s hand resting on the rail instead of holding me down.

A voice outside Leo’s unit said, “There you go.”

At the same time, my medic eased back from my shoulder.

I stared at him.

He stared at me.

Then the last two minutes caught up with me.

“Sorry.”

The medic blinked.

“I’m sorry. I—”

I swallowed.

“I’m sorry.”

From outside, farther down the shoulder, Leo’s voice carried through the open doors of his ambulance.

“Sorry. I’m sorry.”

I closed my eyes.

Of course.

The medic looked over his shoulder toward the road.

Somebody near Leo answered him, too low for me to catch.

I opened my eyes again.

“Is he in?”

“Yeah. He’s locked in.”

“Okay.”

The word came out small.

I flexed my fingers against the cot.

Nothing in me tried to tear the straps apart.

That scared me in a completely different way.

Marisol’s voice carried in through the open rear doors.

“Both in?”

Someone outside answered her.

“Both in.”

“Okay. Anita, I’m with you.”

A few seconds later, a handle clicked without a door opening.

Then clicked again.

The van door opened.

Anita said something too low for me to catch.

Marisol answered, “I’m fine.” Her voice dragged on the second word.

The passenger door shut.

The medic beside me checked one of the straps at my arm.

“Too tight?”

“No.”

“You want it loosened now?”

I looked toward the open doors even though Leo’s ambulance sat out of my sightline from here.

“Leave it.”

“Okay.”

I listened for him.

A door shut somewhere outside.

Then another.

I couldn’t tell if they were his.

“Leo’s in?” I asked again.

“He’s in.”

“Same hospital?”

“Same hospital.”

I let my head rest back against the cot.

“Okay.”

The medic reached for the rear doors.

The first one shut with a heavy metal thump.

The second door closed.
