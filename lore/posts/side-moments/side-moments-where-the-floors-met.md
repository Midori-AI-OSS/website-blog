Grace knocked twice against the doorframe.

Echo looked up from Riley’s hand.

The faded sunflower turned on the green badge reel when Grace stepped into the room. Navy scrubs. Silver wedding band. The same pause at the threshold she had used before the MRI, waiting until Echo was ready to move.

Luna sat near Riley’s pillow with one hand resting in Riley’s hair. The water cup waited on the tray table. Leo had stopped working at the window, his laptop still open beneath his hand. Marisol held the chair nearest the door, notebook braced against one thigh. Her pen moved steadily while the room was quiet enough to catch every stroke.

W.E.A.V.E. drifted inside the clear cup on the blanket shelf.

Riley’s eyes were open.

They had tracked Luna’s face for a few seconds. Now they rested somewhere above her shoulder, the focus thinning again.

Grace smiled at the bed.

Echo smelled wet fabric.

Metal.

Her thumb stopped against Riley’s knuckles.

Grace always arrived lavender-first. In the prep room. On the shuttle. Outside the MRI while Echo changed back into her dress. The smell had stayed close when the medication made everything else harder to hold.

There was no lavender here.

Grace’s eyes moved past Echo.

Wrong color.

Wrong stillness.

Echo’s fear had not reached her yet. The answer came first, clean and cold.

“That isn’t Grace.”

The woman stopped.

Luna’s fingers stilled in Riley’s hair.

Leo lifted his hand from the laptop.

Marisol’s pen kept moving.

Echo stood. Her legs felt steady. That surprised her enough that she noticed it.

“She smells wrong.” Echo met the borrowed eyes. “Her eyes are wrong.”

The cup lid snapped upward.

Bismuth crossed the room in a bright, hard stream.

W.E.A.V.E. filled the open floor between the doorway and Riley’s bed. Gold-green ribbons climbed the walls, spread along the ceiling, and dropped behind the visitor chairs. The room changed shape around her particles. Riley, Echo, Luna, Leo, and Marisol remained together. The Grace-shape stood beyond the moving field.

Echo felt W.E.A.V.E.’s response before the words reached the back of her mind. The sudden density made the room feel smaller and safer at the same time.

__Hold position.__

Grace looked through the barrier.

Past Echo.

Past Riley’s body.

Her attention settled beside the bed.

Her expression warmed.

“There you are, hon.”

The monitor went silent.

The green line continued across the screen. Riley’s chest rose beneath the blanket. Air stirred the curtain beside the window.

The room heard Marisol’s pen.

One fast line. Another underneath it.

Gray opened inside the walls.

The paint stayed beige while distance unfolded through it. The hospital floor stretched beneath itself into a colorless depth. Fayetteville remained outside the window, laid over a flat horizon that reached through the glass.

Grace’s shadow turned sideways across the linoleum.

Luna stood.

“Ethereal.”

The word came from years of study before the rest of the room made sense.

The navy scrub top pulled tight across a chest broken by narrow, wet openings. Dark blood gathered along the fabric. One shoulder shifted higher while the body remained still. The silver ring moved around a finger whose joints changed beneath the skin.

Echo’s certainty held. Fear arrived underneath it now, cold along her arms.

She had trusted this face.

That was the part that hurt.

The thing wearing it looked beside Riley’s bed again.

A woman stood there.

Black off-shoulder sweater. Tan pants. Bare feet over strips of pixel-broken ground that showed through the hospital floor. Her glasses flickered across her face, vanished at the bridge, then returned.

Riley.

Echo forgot to breathe.

The sight of Riley standing should have been the only thing in the room. Echo wanted it to be. Her mind tried to take Riley out of the gray, out of the threat, out of the body lying beneath the blanket.

It could not.

W.E.A.V.E. stood beside her in human form.

Deep-green hair faded toward moss at the ends. A midnight-blue wrap dress fell around a fully resolved interimage. She turned toward the party while her physical particles continued filling the walls and ceiling.

Luna’s understanding arrived all at once.

Layer One had become visible before through B.E.C.C.A.’s display. A narrow view, Riley-controlled, small enough to survive.

This was the whole room.

“Layer One,” Luna said.

Her heart hit hard enough to make the edges of her vision pulse.

The Material room, the gray of the Ethereal Plane, and Riley’s private frame occupied the same space. Every surface had accepted all three.

She found Leo beside her without looking. The bond carried his presence back—steady, alert, afraid.

W.E.A.V.E.’s interimage turned its head.

__Nobody move.__

Her mouth moved with the words.

Marisol’s pen skipped off the line.

She stared at the interimage for half a second, then wrote faster.

W.E.A.V.E. saw the motion too.

<thinking>The room received the send through my field. The interimage articulated it. Both actions belonged to me. Both occupied the same coordinate.</thinking>

Her particles tightened around Riley’s bed.

The Grace-shape moved.

Its arm reached through the gray toward Riley’s body.

Bloody fingers passed between W.E.A.V.E.’s ribbons at an angle that made distance stop behaving. The hand appeared over the bed rail while the shoulder remained near the doorway.

Luna’s focus moved toward her spellbook.

Riley stepped first.

Her bare foot met the hospital floor.

The sound carried through the room.

A soft slap of skin on linoleum.

Echo looked down sharply.

Riley took another step. Pixel-broken ground formed beneath her foot inside Layer One while the hospital floor sounded beneath the same weight.

The Grace-shape smiled.

“You always do that.”

Riley saw its mouth move.

The words reached her through the gray.

She looked at her body in the bed. At the bloody hand extending toward its face. At the thing waiting for her to put herself between them.

It had known where she would stand.

The recognition made her angry before it made her afraid.

She moved in front of the bed.

The hand stopped inches from her.

Her question reached W.E.A.V.E. through the narrow path that had held between them for days.

The Ethereal gray gathered against her palm.

Cold became weight. Smoke folded between her fingers and hardened into a hilt. A dark longsword extended from it, the edge taking the hospital light and giving none of it back.

Leo stared at the blade.

Something about the grip was familiar. The angle. The way the darkness gathered closest to Riley’s hand before running clean along the edge.

The thought struck too close to Luna for him to follow it yet.

The bloody hand reached over Riley’s shoulder.

Riley swung.

The blade crossed the navy scrubs from shoulder to ribs.

Fabric opened.

The body underneath split wider, wet seams pulling away from a depth filled with moving gray. Blood lifted from the wound in slow strings. The Grace-face twisted while the wrong eyes stayed fixed on Riley.

Riley turned the sword and cut again.

The second strike passed through the reaching arm.

The hand fell apart before it reached the bed, fingers separating into blood and gray. The faded sunflower spun away from the torn badge reel and disappeared before it touched the floor.

The thing folded around the wound.

Grace’s mouth opened too far.

The sound that came from it had no place in the hospital or in Layer One. It scraped across the Ethereal depth and made Echo’s teeth hurt.

Riley held the sword ready for another cut.

The borrowed body retreated.

Blood drew backward into the gray. Navy fabric collapsed around the shifting wound-body. The wedding ring remained visible for one beat, circling empty space.

The wrong eyes watched Riley from inside the closing face.

Then they were gone.

The smell left with them.

Riley’s sword loosened into smoke. Darkness slipped between her fingers and returned to the gray.

The alignment stayed open.

Luna watched the doorway settle around empty air. Her body wanted to move toward Riley and toward the bed at the same time. She held where she was because the floors still shared weight and she did not know which movement would pull on what.

Riley turned from the doorway.

She looked toward Luna.

Luna’s heart climbed into her throat.

“Riley.”

Riley watched her mouth.

Then she began walking.

Each bare foot sounded against the linoleum.

Echo listened to every step.

The sound made Riley more real than the sight had. Sight could belong to magic. Sound belonged to the room. The small drag of Riley’s heel belonged to the person Echo had watched walk through kitchens, bars, parking lots, and every other ordinary place that had never seemed precious enough at the time.

Riley stopped in front of Luna.

Her glasses flickered away.

Luna lifted one hand. She wanted to touch Riley and was terrified of finding only gray. Her fingers stayed open between them.

Riley reached first.

She touched the green hair beside Luna’s cheek.

The strands moved beneath her fingers.

Luna jumped.

The contact hit her body before thought. A sharp lift through both shoulders, breath catching, one hand rising toward Riley’s wrist.

Riley’s fingers remained in her hair.

Warm enough to feel.

Solid enough to move the mossy ends against her cheek.

Luna saw Riley’s expression change.

She saw the shape of recognition land without hearing its name.

The pressure behind Luna’s ribs broke.

She stepped forward slowly, giving Riley time to move away.

Riley leaned toward her.

Luna wrapped both arms around her.

The hug went deep. One arm crossed Riley’s back. The other hand settled at the base of her head and entered the black-and-blue hair there. Riley’s sweater bunched beneath Luna’s fingers.

Luna had imagined Riley waking in a hundred small ways. A hand squeezing. A name spoken clearly. A tired complaint about the water.

She had never imagined holding Riley while Riley’s body lay three feet away.

The impossibility made her grip tighter.

“I’ve got you.”

Riley’s arms closed around her.

Luna pressed her face against Riley’s shoulder. The sweater carried no familiar laundry smell, only the faint clean warmth of the frame, but the shape under her hands was Riley.

That was enough to make her eyes burn.

Riley stayed until Luna’s arms eased.

She stepped back.

Luna’s fingers moved through Riley’s hair as the distance opened, following the strands until they slipped from her hand.

Riley turned toward the bed.

Her footsteps carried her back across the small space.

She stood beside her own body.

The face on the pillow looked tired in a way Riley could understand from the outside. A strand of black hair crossed one eye. The blue ends had caught beneath the pillow and curled against the cheek.

Riley gathered the loose strand between two fingers.

The hair moved.

Echo made a small, broken sound.

Riley drew the strand away from her body’s face. She freed the blue ends from beneath the pillow and smoothed them back.

It was such an ordinary gesture that Leo had to look toward the window for one second.

The fear had been easier to hold while Riley carried a sword.

This was Riley caring for herself because no one else had noticed the hair in her face.

His throat tightened.

Riley placed two fingers against one eyelid and lowered it.

Then the other.

Her body’s lashes settled against her cheeks.

The monitor continued its silent rhythm above them.

Riley looked at the sleeping face for another moment. Her expression stayed small, but her shoulders lowered.

She turned toward Leo.

Her bare feet sounded between them.

Leo faced her with his injured wrist held close to his body. He wanted to say something useful. Something that could carry the hospital, the days of waiting, and the fact that she had just crossed a room that should never have existed.

Nothing useful came.

Riley stopped in front of him.

She nodded.

Relief moved through Leo so sharply that the bond carried part of it to Luna.

He returned the nod.

“Hi, Riley.”

Riley watched his mouth.

The corner of hers moved.

It was barely a smile. Leo took it anyway.

Riley turned toward Echo.

Echo remained beside the bed.

Her fingers had curled into the edge of the blanket. She released them when Riley approached, then caught the fabric again.

Riley took two steps and stopped beside her own arm.

Her gaze dropped to the clear tubing taped near her wrist.

Echo followed it.

Riley reached down.

She peeled the tape loose and pulled the IV free.

Blood welled immediately.

Riley caught a folded gauze square from the tray and pressed it over the puncture. Red ran around the edge before she got pressure on it, crossing her fingers and the heel of her hand.

She held it there for two breaths.

Then she folded the gauze over the spot and pressed the loose tape back across it.

Her fingers were still wet with blood when she straightened.

She looked at Echo.

She opened her arms.

Echo stared.

The invitation was unmistakable.

Her fear scattered. What remained was the distance between them.

She crossed it.

Echo reached Riley hard enough to shift both of them.

Her arms closed around Riley’s ribs. One hand flattened between Riley’s shoulder blades. The other caught the back of the black sweater and held.

Riley folded around her.

Echo pressed her face into Riley’s shoulder.

The sweater was soft against her cheek.

“I’m here.”

Riley’s arms tightened.

The words came harder after that.

“I’m still here.”

Echo felt Riley’s hand settle against her back.

Days of touching a motionless body had taught her every kind of contact that did not answer.

This answered.

She held Riley with everything she had left.
